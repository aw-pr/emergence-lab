## Harness settings

128×128 grid, channel 0 (Phase), 400 warmup steps, flux gap 6, coverage
threshold 0.5, seed 1 pinned.

**The single channel is a phase, and phase wraps.** Cells at 0.99 and 0.01 are
neighbours on the circle but maximally distant to a linear metric, so spatial
autocorrelation reads a wrap-around boundary as a discontinuity and temporal flux
reads a phase rolling past 1.0 as a full-scale jump. This flatters regimes whose
wavefronts happen to sit away from 0 and penalises otherwise-identical ones whose
do not. Scores here rank **within this sim only**, and even within it they carry
more noise than the other eleven sweeps in this stage. A circular-statistics
metric (mean resultant length, circular autocorrelation) is the right instrument
and is out of scope for this stage — recorded as follow-up.

## Reading

The sweep splits cleanly on coupling mode, with no overlap between the two
groups. **The twenty local-coupling sets span 0.541–0.800; the twenty
global-coupling sets span 0.058–0.442.** Local
coupling builds spatially organised phase waves and vortices — autocorrelation
0.78–0.95 — while global coupling pulls every oscillator toward one shared phase
regardless of where it sits on the grid, leaving a spatially uniform field with
autocorrelation 0.01.

**That is not a defect in the two global presets.** Global coupling locking the
field is the phenomenon those presets exist to demonstrate; "Global lock" is
doing precisely what its name says. The composite has no way to reward a
correct demonstration of uniformity — a uniform field is indistinguishable from
a dead one on entropy, structure and flux alike. Recorded here so a later sweep
does not "fix" them.

Among the local presets, Vortex field (0.787) is essentially at the ceiling: the
best swept set beats it by 0.013 (1.7%), inside the metric's noise for a wrapping
channel. Local phase waves (0.755) is the highest-entropy frame in the entire
run (1.00 — a perfectly flat phase histogram) and trades some structure for it.

### Evidence selections, validation and interpretation

The manifest’s fixed selection rule chooses c28 (highest local texture score), c38 (global R closest to 0.5), and c61 (highest global R). They are evidence selections, not three promotion recommendations. c38 has R=0.099589 at the selection time, still 0.400411 away from 0.5: its selection is not evidence of a middle-order regime.

At seed 1, c28 scores 0.783126 against Vortex field’s 0.787109 and Local phase waves’ 0.755477. Across seeds, that small ordering is not stable: c28 scores 0.783126/0.788844/0.784732 while Vortex field scores 0.787109/0.785136/0.782219. Three seeds are robustness observations, not a distributional guarantee.

Manual visual findings from the time strips: c28 retains broad diagonal phase bands at 400–800, then breaks into more irregular patches at 1600. The app views resemble the existing Local phase waves reference; there is no convincing new visual regime to promote. The motion evidence supports evolving/deforming wave structure, not a measured rigid travelling speed. c38 is a fine red/blue speckle field at all four app times, consistent with low global order and low neighbour coherence. c61 is a nearly uniform locked field with faint residual variation, visually similar to Global lock; this is a valid synchronisation outcome despite its low texture score. Vortex field retains a clearer broad red/blue vortex-like arrangement. These descriptions are visual readings, not numerical class labels.

No robust new partially synchronised state is established by this grid. At 128², c38 R at 1600 is 0.131322/0.016491/0.058674 for seeds 1/7/23, while c61 remains 0.991111/0.991015/0.990999. Locked fields can look stationary while their common phase drifts. Short-gap P≈1 does not prove zero motion. All three behaviours remain in the evidence, including rejected examples.

The unchanged composite is unreliable as a cross-regime phase preference: c61 at seed 7 has score 0 even though R≈0.991, and Global lock has score 0 for seeds 7 and 23 with R≈0.999. Its linear threshold/coverage, histogram and wrap-sensitive covariance change with absolute phase. High R describes synchronisation, not aesthetic interest; low R can describe either coherent balanced waves or unrelated speckle, so C and visual comparison are necessary. The grayscale black/white seam is a phase-coordinate wrap, not a physical edge.

All 261 CPU readings (69 base + 192 validation) have non-finite, out-of-range and exact lower/upper endpoint fractions 0. The 32 GPU captures have non-finite/out-of-range fractions 0, upper endpoint fraction 0 and lower endpoint fractions from 0 to 0.0000022605613425925925 (one cell in the largest observed case). A wrapped phase endpoint is not density saturation. Kuramoto occupies the whole periodic domain: occupied-border fraction is 1 and off-frame particle clipping is N/A. No object is being fitted/cropped; full-field edge crossings are periodic continuation. Manual views show no blank, blown-out or clipped-support pathology.
