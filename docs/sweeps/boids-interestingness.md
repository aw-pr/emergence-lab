## Context: the 2026-07-16 null result this sweep answers

The Gray-Scott write-up recorded the original boids sweep as a null result:
*"the occupancy-field lens is the wrong instrument for a sparse swarm — all
sets score ≈0.02 because binary point-density on a 200² grid carries almost no
spatial-autocorrelation signal."* It named the fix: flocking "interest" lives
in velocity coherence (the kernel's vx/vy channels 2–3), not occupancy
texture. Stage 63 built both instruments that write-up and the Particle Life
one asked for, and this is the boids re-run under them. This is the **first
run of the new instrument: a calibration run. No preset was changed and none
of these numbers justifies a promotion on its own.**

## The instrument

Boids opts into the point-cloud path (`PointCloudConfig` in
`e2e/harness/sims.ts`; point-cloud section of `e2e/harness/metrics.ts`):

- **Smoothed density.** Both frames pass through a toroidal separable Gaussian
  blur, `blurRadius: 6` (sigma 3) — between the separationRadius axis (5–12)
  and the smallest visualRadius (16), so a flock blurs into one graded blob
  without bridging separate flocks. Both frames are rescaled by one shared
  maximum, so temporal flux stays comparable.
- **Smoothed-field coverage threshold 0.25**, set from a measured probe (the
  raw 0.001 dot-occupancy threshold misreads a smoothed field): probed
  flocking regimes read coverage 0.46–0.77, inside the composite's plateau,
  while a fully collapsed flock reads ~0.07.
- **Velocity coherence**: the polarisation order parameter over the rasterised
  vx/vy channels (2–3), reported *beside* the composite, never folded into it.
  1 means one coherent flock; ~0 means directions cancel.
- **obstacleLayout pinned to `"none"`**: the default reef raster paints
  constant density and pseudo-velocity tones into the state channels, which
  would feed a fixed vector bias into every candidate's coherence. This also
  restores the 2026-07-16 conditions, which predate obstacles.

## Mechanics of the run

The full sweep drives each of the 62 kernels three times (density + vx + vy),
which outran the global 180 s Playwright test budget on 2026-08-30 (it died at
roughly 50 of 60 sets). Fixed by **raising the timeout for this one test
in-spec** (`test.setTimeout(900_000)` inside the `sweep boids` test in
`e2e/sweep.spec.ts`) rather than splitting the run; the sweep completed
headless in 4.3 minutes, well inside the ceiling.

## Calibration notes, recorded honestly

- **The blur trades one saturation for another, exactly as it did for
  Particle Life.** Raw occupancy pinned the structure term at ≈0; the smoothed
  field pins it at 0.964–1.000 for all 62 runs — a blurred field is smooth by
  construction, so spatial autocorrelation *still* does not rank boids. What
  changed is that **entropy now reads the flock texture** (0.523–0.889 across
  sets) and **coverage reads concentration** (0.174–0.948), and those two now
  carry the ranking.
- **Velocity coherence actually discriminates.** It spans 0.199–0.996 across
  the swept sets (mean 0.897; 14 of 60 sets below 0.9). The low readings are
  the fragmenting regimes — small visualRadius with strong, wide separation
  (minimum: visualRadius 16, separation 0.55, separationRadius 12 at 0.199) —
  where the swarm splinters into many counter-moving flocks whose vectors
  cancel. Both shipped presets read ≈0.99: they are single coherent flocks,
  and on this axis they are indistinguishable.
- **The composite's low scores are coverage verdicts.** The bottom three sets
  (0.311–0.629) are low-separation gas regimes whose smoothed field covers
  0.90–0.95 of the grid — past the plateau's top edge — and the Tight flock
  reference (0.429) is the opposite failure, a collapsed blob covering 0.072.
  The instrument reads both as extreme concentration/dispersal, which is the
  designed behaviour, but note the aesthetic call is genuinely arguable for a
  tight flock; that is what the calibration framing is for.

## Does the new instrument separate the shipped presets?

**Yes, decisively.** Under the 2026-07-16 raw stack every set — presets
included — scored ≈0.02, a spread of effectively zero. Under the smoothed
instrument the two shipped presets score **0.918 and 0.429 — a spread of
0.489**, and the full swept range spans 0.634 (0.311–0.945). The separation is
real signal, not noise in a constant: Balanced flock sits in the plateau with
high entropy, while Tight flock is read as a collapsed blob (coverage 0.072,
entropy 0.361). The velocity-coherence axis adds what the composite cannot
see: it cleanly separates coherent flocking (≈0.99) from splintered
counter-flows (down to 0.199), which is the dimension the original write-up
said flocking interest actually lives on.

## Promotion

**None.** First run of a new instrument is calibration: record, do not act.
Two further reasons to act slowly here beyond the calibration rule: the
composite's harsh verdict on Tight flock is an aesthetic judgement call, not a
settled fact; and boids has prior form — a stage-15 regime change passed
numerically but stalled on its browser check for a full re-brief cycle, so any
future boids recommendation must go through the visual/browser gate, not
numbers alone.

### Interpretation

**No numeric winner is a promotion.** The top score (0.923, alignment 0.25 / cohesion 0 / 12 flocks) beats the shipped Balanced flock (0.918) by 0.005, inside the spread the validation shows for the same preset across times (0.873–0.918) and resolutions (0.882). Diversity was applied to the finalists: all three have cohesion 0 and differ in alignment and initialFlocks, because cohesion 0 dominates the top of the ranking — **zero cohesion ranks first in the retained top five**, and any non-zero cohesion is far more variable: cohesion 0 scores 0.886–0.923 (mean 0.907), while 0.03/0.04/0.05 span 0.17–0.87 (means 0.550/0.465/0.379), the lowest sets (0.170–0.178) being collapsed flocks the instrument penalises through coverage.

Velocity coherence separates alignment cleanly (0.865–0.881 at alignment 0.01 vs 0.98+ at ≥ 0.15) while the composite barely moves, so the composite is not what distinguishes these regimes — the retained `velocityCoherence` column is.

Manual visual finding (grayscale kernel-field PNGs only, 2500 boids on 200² (the historical calibration, not the shipped 17 777), `../images/sweeps-2026-10-30/`): at all three finalists and at the Balanced reference the raw field reads as a sparse speckle with no visible flock hull, filament or stream; the three separated frames of each finalist are not visually distinguishable from each other or from Balanced flock by eye. The metric differences are therefore not visible in these renders. This is the same dot-raster limit the 2026-09-01 write-up recorded; app renderer screenshots are in the supplement below.

Tight flock (shipped) scores 0.429 at 220 steps (coverage 0.072, the collapsed-flock floor), falls to 0.315–0.350 at longer horizons and recovers to 0.637 on the 300² grid: its score is a function of grid and horizon, which the earlier 2026-09-01 reading had already flagged as the instrument's penalty for concentration, not a defect finding.

Obstacle-bearing frames (visual comparison, not ranked against the obstacle-free scores): breakwaters, rocks and reef, at the default obstacleAmount 0.5, 440 steps, for each finalist and for Balanced flock. In the reef frames the obstacles are painted as large constant-grey strips and discs, and the flock in the retained frame is piled against the top edge of the grid. Fixed obstacle raster is not flock coherence and no coherence number is claimed for these frames. Frames: finalist 1 reef, Balanced reef; all layouts are listed in the retained JSON (`finalists[].obstacleVisual`, `referenceValidation`). Whether the top-edge pile-up is an obstacle interaction or boundary behaviour was not isolated here.

## 2026-11-03 — clump sweep (stage 152)

The operator rejected the three stage-144 Boids trials on 2026-11-03 and asked for "faster with multiple clumps going in different directions". Stage 144 could not find that regime: it pinned `maxSpeed` at 16, every finalist had cohesion 0, and its composite rewards even coverage. ### Method and instrument

## 2026-11-04 — unbiased neighbour sampling (stage 153)

The binned kernel now counts and flattens its cells into reusable buffers, then takes at most 72 evenly spaced candidates across the combined nine-cell neighbourhood. A seed-, boid- and step-dependent hash chooses the sample phase. Every candidate has the same inclusion probability; cells contribute in proportion to their population, and reaching an accepted-neighbour count no longer ends the scan on one side. All candidates are tested against the existing toroidal visual and separation radii. Sparse neighbourhoods visit every candidate once.

After the existing steering and upper speed bound, the binned path raises nonzero velocities below 0.7 × maxSpeed to that speed without changing their heading. These are the only new tuning constants: `NEIGHBOUR_CANDIDATES = 72` and `MIN_SPEED_FRACTION = 0.7`. The brute-force path, wander, parameter schema, defaults, presets, initial flock assignment, obstacles and renderer interface are unchanged. New buffers are allocated in init, reused during steps and released in destroy. The nine-entry neighbourhood scratch buffer is reused for the lifetime of the kernel.

**The stage-144 and stage-152 Boids readings predate this change and remain historical evidence.** Their frames, scores and presets have not been regenerated or re-scored.

## 2026-11-13 - flow sweep (heading variety that lasts, per device tier)

The operator, 2026-11-13: "I think maybe the large visual and spatial radius destroys the effect a little on smaller screens and can be a bit clunky with that many boids. Run a tuning sweep on it ... some presets for each candidate in the browser I can quickly judge. Colour in Boids is heading. The WebGL glyph shader (`boidPaletteColour` in `src/app/webglRenderer.ts`) maps each boid's heading onto the cyclic twilight palette and dims boids that oppose the global mean heading. "Colourful" therefore means many headings on screen at once, in coherent flowing patches, kept for minutes: not one shared heading (monochrome), not per-boid random headings (speckle), not tight balls on an empty screen. This sweep searched for that per device tier, on each tier's real compute grid, and hands over a shortlist as an importable presets file. No default, preset, kernel or test was changed.

### Method and instrument

The instrument, its formulas, calibration and limits are in [`data/2026-11-13/boids-flow/INSTRUMENT.md`](data/2026-11-13/boids-flow/INSTRUMENT.md) (method `boids-flow-v1`, script `scripts/sweep-boids-flow.mjs`). In short:

- The real kernel (`.test-build`, compiled from `src/sims/boids/kernel.ts`) runs offline on the tier's measured grid at the app's per-frame step, dt = 1/60: 60 steps per app-second, so 3 app-minutes is 10800 steps. The 144, 152 and 153 harnesses stepped dt = 1, which is why the pre-11-05 defaults read "aligned by step 300" there; at the app's dt the same row reads global order 0.47-0.52 at 20 s.
- Per sample it reads global order G (1 = one heading), local order L (coherent patches against speckle), heading entropy E, heading domains, coverage, the densest-5% share (tight balls), and median nearest-neighbour spacing in CSS px (fineness). `flowScore = L x E x (1 - G) x coverageTerm x patchTerm`.
- **Chaos lifetime** (per seed) is the last sample from 5 s on with every `flowScore >= 0.5`; reported in app-seconds at 60 steps a second. `rowScore` is the minimum `flowScore` from 10 s on. Long results rank by worst-seed lifetime, then median.
- Stages: a desktop pilot (14 rows, 2 seeds, 40 s; long pilot 5 rows, 3 min) to calibrate; a screen per tier (96 Latin-hypercube candidates plus references, seed 0, 40 s); a long stage per tier (top screen rows plus references, seeds 0-2, 3 min); supplementary long rounds that filled two gaps in the long selection; uncontended step timing; then real-renderer captures of the candidates.
- Captures (`scripts/capture-boids-flow.mjs`, record `captures.json`): each row opened as a preset in headless Chromium under its tier's emulation, with trails 0.93, bloom 0.3 and twilight as shipped, kernel seed and dt pinned by an init script, frames held at 5, 20, 60 and 150 s. At the first target the live kernel matched a fresh kernel stepped the same way bit for bit (`seedCheck` identical) on every row, so the frames are the offline seed runs.
- Judging: one judge per tier and one skeptic across tiers read the contact sheets, individual frames and the data.

### What the data says matters

1. **Alignment, on every tier.** Rows still multi-heading at 40 s had median alignment 0.045-0.062; collapsed rows 0.067-0.074. Cutting alignment on the same row moved the worst seed from 30 to 60 s on desktop (0.076 to 0.04), 45 to 60 s on tablet (0.031 to 0.015) and 20 to 30 s on phone (0.035 to 0.015).
2. **A separation radius above the visual radius speeds the collapse.** On desktop the median ratio was 0.87 in rows that held and 1.19 in rows that collapsed. The kernel applies separation only to neighbours inside the visual radius and sums the push, so today's 27 against 14 pushes every visible neighbour hard. That gives the even, coarse 7.6 CSS px lattice that alignment then sweeps into one heading.
3. **Smaller radii on smaller grids.** On tablet and phone, rows that held had visual radius 8-10 against 10-13 for rows that collapsed, and 3.6-4.7 boids within a visual radius against 5-8. More flocks (9-10 against 8) and slightly more cohesion (0.011-0.016 against 0.009-0.010) helped there. Speed hardly mattered.
4. **Keep radii in absolute cells; do not scale them to the screen.** Desktop Loose drift scaled to about 1% of the short side lasts 20 s on tablet and clumps from the start on phone; today's 14 / 27 scaled to the short side lasts 10 s; Scatter's radius 24 lasts 60 s on desktop but 20 s on tablet and phone. Visual radius 5 (0.5%, 0.9% and 1.3% of the short side) holds on all three grids.
5. **Fineness and persistence need not trade off.** Small radii with light alignment are both finer and longer-lived than today. The pre-11-05 family is finer still inside its clumps but clumpy and collapsing on every grid.

The fineness floor is the grid, not the params: point size 4 is a 2-cell glyph radius, 6 CSS px on the desktop cap grid, whatever the spacing.

**The rule that links the tiers.** Fine schools is one recipe in absolute cells: visual radius 5, separation radius 8, alignment 0.015, cohesion about 0.018, speed 24, separation 0.191, 9 flocks, point size 4, with the boid count at about 0.024 boids a cell capped at the schema's 28000. That gives 28000 on desktop (the cap, 0.0171 a cell on 1707x960), 9750 on tablet (0.0238) and 7750 on phone (0.0235). Uncertainty: the phone row that was run uses separation radius 9, separation 0.326 and speed 26; the literal tablet set has not been run on the phone grid. A count that follows the tier, as `tierParamOverridesFor` does today, does not follow a desktop window's grid: 28000 boids on a 1280x720 window is 0.030 a cell, 1.78 times the tested density.

**What opening a preset does on each tier** (read in `src/app/simView.ts`, `src/app/renderer.ts`, `src/app/qualityProfiles.ts`; proved below):

- A loaded snapshot's params replace the factory params, so `tierParamOverridesFor` is not applied: the preset's `boidCount` is used as written on any device. The iPad opened the tablet preset at 9750, not the tablet opening's 7000.
- The snapshot's resolution replaces the tier default, so the tablet and phone cap to balanced is not applied either. Opened on the iPad, the desktop preset ran at ultra on a 1180x820 grid with 28000 boids: 0.029 boids a cell, 1.7 times the desktop tier's density and 2.4 times the tablet grid's cells. Open each tier's own presets on that device.
- The grid is the canvas's CSS size (one cell per CSS px for Boids), scaled down to the preset's cell budget, and is fixed at load: a plain resize keeps it. So a preset reproduces its tier's grid only on that tier's canvas size. Tablet presets give 768x534 in a 1180x820 window; in a 1280x720 window they give 853x480 (the same 410k cells, but 16:9 instead of 1.44:1). Phone presets give 390x844 only in a 390x844 viewport, which needs a device-emulation viewport (for example the browser's device toolbar at 390x844) on a desktop. Desktop presets give 1707x960 only on a canvas of at least 1.64M CSS px at 16:9; a 1280x720 window gives 1280x720 and a MacBook Pro 16 default window 1592x1029. Set the window first, then open the preset.
- A Boids preset cannot carry a seed: `PresetValidator` accepts `seed` only for Physarum and Particle Life. Every open draws a fresh random seed, so the operator will see seeds other than the photographed seed 0, and seed variance is large (desktop Fine schools lasts 60, 75 and 135 s across three seeds).
- Imported presets live in that browser's storage only. On the iPad the file has to reach the device (Files) and be imported on the Presets page of the same app build. Importing the file twice adds "(copy)" names.
- Steering is applied once per rendered frame. Today's desktop runs at about 32 fps; a cheaper default will run faster, and a 120 Hz iPad or phone runs about twice as many steps a second as these 60 steps-a-second lifetimes assume.
