## Method

The pre-measurement `manifest.json` declares exact candidates in expansion order, fixed parameters, source hashes, prior coverage and reproduction settings. Complete ranked results, references and validation retain full parameters and method version `eca-spacetime-v1`. Raw census fields retain every A/B binary field losslessly as zlib-compressed uint8 bytes encoded in base64. `inflateSync(Buffer.from(value, "base64"))` recovers the 128×128 row-major cells. Validation rows retain the same encoding for larger histories.

The existing browser driver loads the real kernel. All census candidates use width=height=128, `stepsPerFrame=1`, `dt=1`, 128 generated rows including generation zero, and a one-generated-row flux gap. Initialization writes one row: 127 step calls fill the history. Newest rows are at the top. No blank allocation padding is scored. A zero generated by the rule is legitimate data. All cells are thresholded at >0.5 for the three added diagnostics:

- **Row entropy:** mean base-2 binary Shannon entropy across rows, in bits [0,1]. It measures each row's occupancy balance, not spatial randomness.
- **Row change:** fraction of differing cells between adjacent rows, averaged over height−1 transitions. High values can describe a trivial alternating pattern.
- **Repeated-row fraction:** fraction of rows after the first whose full binary row appeared earlier, denominator height−1. This detects exact repetition within this window; it neither estimates a universal period nor proves its absence outside the window.

These are heuristic Wolfram-class signals, not automatic class labels or proofs of complexity. The old histogram entropy, spatial autocorrelation, coverage, temporal flux and composite score are retained unchanged. The added diagnostics do not feed the legacy score. Binary histogram entropy has a different scale from the new row entropy. Legacy score explicitly remains a screening aid, with strong bias towards coherent solid regions.

The shipped Rule 184 reference uses two rows per step. Its parameters are preserved exactly: the opt-in clock allocates one extra history row, makes 64 calls, and crops generation 128 to recover generations 0…127. The B frame uses generations 1…128 from that same batch. Thus speed does not change the compared history or flux interval. A supplemental regression proves equality against single-row stepping at 128, 129, 256 and 1024 generated rows. Generic driver behaviour is unchanged when this clock is absent.

The kernel has a periodic horizontal boundary. Histories are finite rolling windows; losing old rows is intentional temporal cropping, not spatial clipping. Non-finite/out-of-range fractions and both channel endpoint fractions are retained for every census field and each validation frame. Binary endpoints are expected, not saturation failures. The grid kernel produces no off-frame spatial coordinates, so spatial clipping is zero; periodic wraparound and finite-width extinction can still dominate the result.

## Searched and not searched

Rules 0…255 × seedMode {0,1,2}, in rule-outer/seed-inner order, with no equivalence deduplication: 768 candidates. Seed 0 is a centre cell; seed 1 is deterministic `coordinateHash(x)<0.22`; seed 2 repeats `011010`. There is no user-controlled RNG seed. Seed replication is therefore inapplicable. Changing width changes the initial row, including the coordinate hash domain and stripe seam.

The [batch live audit](2026-10-30-interestingness-batch.md#live-audit) records no prior ECA sweep config, grid, appendix range or retained ranking. The [kernel schema](../../src/sims/elementary-cellular-automata/kernel.ts) supplies the complete rule and seed ranges. The [shipped presets](../../src/app/presets.ts) supply all current references. Candidate overlap with these references is intentional in an exhaustive census and is not claimed as novelty. No historical config or result was replaced.

No custom initial rows, arbitrary RNG seeds, speed axis, palette axis or automatic Wolfram classification was searched. Each raw census row annotates its rule's mirror, state-complement conjugate and combined transform, plus the smallest rule number in that rule-table orbit. Mirror reverses left/right neighbourhood bits. Complement maps output at neighbourhood n to `1−output(7−n)`; it is not merely `255−rule`. Equivalent rule tables can produce different outputs on the same untransformed seed. Labels remain separate; these annotations do not establish independent classes or deduplicate evidence.

Three selections get 256-wide/256-high histories at 256,384,512 and1024 generated rows: 12 longer-history candidate settings. The legacy score leader gets one extra 256-wide/1024-row negative check. Total 13 of at most 24 longer-history candidate settings, plus the four current references at identical settings. Repeated measurements and calibration references are additional, not new searches.

## Proposed promotions

No production promotion is made. Rule 54/noise is the strongest proposal for operator review because its small interacting triangular patches and ordered background look different from the existing single-seed Rule 110 wedge. Rule 106/noise is retained as an irregular banded alternative, without asserting aesthetic superiority to Rule 30. Rule 57/single cell is retained as an ordered contrast, without a promotion recommendation. The solid-wedge score leader is rejected. Existing Rule 30,90,110 and184 remain useful distinct references; no replacement quota is imposed.

The three selections were chosen across behaviour appearances, not one per seed mode and not the three largest scores. Their low legacy ranks are intentional. Width256 and longer histories test persistence on one larger finite torus; they are not infinite-lattice guarantees. No stochastic robustness or formal universality claim is made.
