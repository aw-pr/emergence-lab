## Harness settings

128×128 grid, channel 0 (Spin), 300 warmup steps, flux gap 4, coverage threshold
0.5, seed 7 pinned on every set.

**The spin field is two-valued, so entropy is structurally pinned at ~0.20 for
every set** — a binary field can fill only 2 of the metric's 32 histogram bins,
and log2(2)/log2(32) = 0.2. The detail term therefore contributes a near-constant
0.09 to every score, and the whole ranking is carried by spatial autocorrelation
and temporal flux. That is the correct reading for a domain-coarsening model
(the question is how organised the domains are and whether they are still
evolving), but it means Ising scores are not comparable in magnitude to a
continuous-field sim's.

Coverage at threshold 0.5 is the fraction of up spins, so it sits near 0.5 for any
balanced state and only leaves the coverageFactor plateau when the lattice tips.

rather than falling off a cliff. Sorting all 32 sets by that ratio:

The peak sits at T/J ≈ 1.3, on the ordered side of the 2D Ising critical ratio
2.269, where large domains have formed and are still slowly coarsening. From
there structure bleeds away and flux rises as thermal noise takes over: the worst
swept sets are T 3.54–4.0 at J 0.6 (0.179–0.184, autocorrelation 0.16–0.17, flux
0.46–0.47) — pure static.

Three of the four presets place sensibly on that curve. **Critical domains
(0.457) scores below Cold quench (0.564) and that is correct behaviour, not a
defect**: at exactly T=2.269 the model is meant to sit on the transition, with
domains constantly forming and dissolving (flux 0.1747, the second-highest in
the run). The composite's liveliness term is a gentle 0.85–1.0 multiplier and
cannot reward that as much as the structure term rewards Cold quench's frozen
domains. Both are doing their job.

## The defect: "Positive field" saturates to a blank lattice

`externalField: 0.35` at T 1.8 drives coverage to **0.990** — 99% of spins have
been pulled to the same state — with entropy 0.02 and autocorrelation 0.07. The
lattice is a flat colour. This is exactly what the parameter's own help text
warns about ("away from zero it steadily tips the board towards all-up or
all-down"), and it is grid- and seed-independent physics rather than a sweep
artefact: a uniform bias with no opposing term always wins eventually.

## Appendix: multi-lag structure term cross-check — 2026-08-30

Stage 66 added `multiLagSpatialAutocorrelation` (`e2e/harness/metrics.ts`), a
generalisation of the existing lag-1 spatial autocorrelation across lags
{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8} that reports the strongest reading found at any of them. Full
write-up and the Game of Life rescue case are in
`docs/sweeps/game-of-life-interestingness.md`. This is the cross-check this card
requires against a sim already flagged as the term behaving *correctly*
(Critical domains vs Cold quench, above) — Ising's domains are large coarsened
blobs, not fine-scale periodic structure, so no rescue is expected here.

| reference | lag-1 (existing) | multi-lag (new) | disagreement |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Critical domains | 0.6677 | 0.6677 | none |
| Cold quench | 0.9600 | 0.9600 | none |
| Hot noise | 0.2394 | 0.2394 | none |

None. All three references read identically under both terms — Ising's domain
walls have no periodicity at the lags searched, so the max-over-lags reading
collapses to the lag-1 reading exactly. This confirms the new term does not
disturb a case the write-up already established as correct: Critical domains
still scores below Cold quench under either term, for the same physical
reason given above.
