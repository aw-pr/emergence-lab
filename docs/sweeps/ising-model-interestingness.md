# 2D Ising Model interestingness sweep — 2026-08-23

> **Evidence files.** Sweep evidence (ranked JSON, manifests, screenshots) is not kept in the repository; the harness writes it to git-ignored folders. File names below are plain text so they can be matched against a local run.

Generated by the Playwright interestingness-sweep harness (`e2e/`). Run it with:

```bash
SWEEP=1 npx playwright test sweep.spec.ts -g "sweep ising"
```

Artifacts land under `e2e/artifacts/ising-model/` (git-ignored). The config is
`ISING_MODEL` in `e2e/harness/sims.ts`. Metric stack unchanged — see
`docs/sweeps/gray-scott-interestingness.md`.

## Harness settings

128×128 grid, channel 0 (Spin), 300 warmup steps, flux gap 4, coverage threshold
0.5, seed 7 pinned on every set.

**The spin field is two-valued, so entropy is structurally pinned at ~0.20 for
every set** — a binary field can fill only 2 of the metric's 32 histogram bins,
and log2(2)/log2(32) = 0.2. The detail term therefore contributes a near-constant
0.09 to every score, and the whole ranking is carried by spatial autocorrelation
and temporal flux. That is the correct reading for a domain-coarsening model
(the question is how organised the domains are and whether they are still
evolving), but it means Ising scores are not comparable in magnitude to a
continuous-field sim's.

Coverage at threshold 0.5 is the fraction of up spins, so it sits near 0.5 for any
balanced state and only leaves the coverageFactor plateau when the lattice tips.

## Reading

The swept ranking is a clean diagonal in T/J, and it declines monotonically
rather than falling off a cliff. Sorting all 32 sets by that ratio:

| T/J | score | autocorr | flux |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.57 | 0.564 | 0.95 | 0.013 |
| 1.33 | **0.586** | 0.93 | 0.028 |
| 2.14 | 0.499 | 0.75 | 0.129 |
| 2.85 | 0.335 | 0.45 | 0.317 |
| 4.42 | 0.229 | 0.25 | 0.426 |
| 5.90 | 0.184 | 0.17 | 0.458 |

The peak sits at T/J ≈ 1.3, on the ordered side of the 2D Ising critical ratio
2.269, where large domains have formed and are still slowly coarsening. From
there structure bleeds away and flux rises as thermal noise takes over: the worst
swept sets are T 3.54–4.0 at J 0.6 (0.179–0.184, autocorrelation 0.16–0.17, flux
0.46–0.47) — pure static.

Three of the four presets place sensibly on that curve. **Critical domains
(0.457) scores below Cold quench (0.564) and that is correct behaviour, not a
defect**: at exactly T=2.269 the model is meant to sit on the transition, with
domains constantly forming and dissolving (flux 0.1747, the second-highest in
the run). The composite's liveliness term is a gentle 0.85–1.0 multiplier and
cannot reward that as much as the structure term rewards Cold quench's frozen
domains. Both are doing their job.

## The defect: "Positive field" saturates to a blank lattice

`externalField: 0.35` at T 1.8 drives coverage to **0.990** — 99% of spins have
been pulled to the same state — with entropy 0.02 and autocorrelation 0.07. The
lattice is a flat colour. This is exactly what the parameter's own help text
warns about ("away from zero it steadily tips the board towards all-up or
all-down"), and it is grid- and seed-independent physics rather than a sweep
artefact: a uniform bias with no opposing term always wins eventually.

## Promotion

**None — escalated to the operator.** The top swept set beats the best incumbent
by 0.022 (3.9%), well inside judgement territory, so there is no promotion to
make on the healthy presets.

"Positive field" is a genuine defect with a delta big enough to act on, but
**this sweep produced no candidate for it**, because `externalField` was one of
the three params held fixed. Promoting any of the swept sets would mean setting
`externalField: 0`, which deletes the only thing that preset demonstrates.

`externalField ∈ [0.02, 0.2]` at T 1.8–2.4 to find the largest bias that still
leaves visible domain structure at 300 steps. That is a narrow, cheap sweep and
the honest way to fix this preset with evidence.

### Method

Fixed steps 100, 300 and 900 on 128², flux gap 4, coverage threshold 0.5. Mean of the three readings is the ranked score. Fixed for every set: externalField 0, sweepsPerStep 0.5, seed 7. The ranked score is the unchanged `scoreFrames` composite averaged over the captures (`summarizeMetrics`); no new metric or weight was introduced. Diagnostics are retained beside the score and never feed it: nonFiniteFraction (0 on every row) and endpointFraction are retained in the JSON; `absMagnetisation` is |2·(up fraction) − 1|; `domainWallDensity` is the share of periodic neighbour pairs that differ. Kernel steps are fully deterministic given the explicit seed (7 for ranking; 7, 11 and 23 for finalists and references).

### Interpretation

Scores shortlist; they do not decide which lattice looks better. What the evidence shows:

- **Initial state decides the outcome when it is ordered.** Every `up` start is a pathological ranking tail: at T/J 2.35 and below the lattice stays nearly uniform (|m| 0.48 to 0.99, score 0.001 to 0.362) because the captured steps at 0.5 sweeps are not enough to nucleate flipped domains from a ferromagnetic start. From T/J 2.5 up to 3.3 up-start scores catch up with random and checkerboard (0.372 against 0.392 at 2.5; 0.288 against 0.287 at 3.3), as expected once the thermal noise exceeds what the ordered start can hold. These rows are retained and not promoted.
- **Random and checkerboard starts rank within 0.012 of each other at every ratio** (for example 0.580 against 0.570 at 1.4, 0.460 against 0.450 at 2.25). The checkerboard start (a maximally frustrated state for a ferromagnet) does not open a new regime at this horizon; it relaxes to the same domain statistics.
- **Near criticality the score falls smoothly with T/J**, from 0.580 at 1.4 through 0.481 at 2.2, 0.460 at 2.25 and 0.423 at 2.35 to 0.288 at 3.3. There is no peak at the critical ratio: domain-wall density rises monotonically (0.04 at 1.4, 0.15 at 2.2, 0.19 at 2.35) and flux rises with it, which this instrument penalises. The critical-domains reference (T/J 2.269, 0.460) sits on the same smooth trend as the neighbouring 2.25 and 2.3 sets (0.450 to 0.460 and 0.441 to 0.448).
- **Symmetry control.** The best `up` set (c32, T 2.25, J 0.9, ratio 2.5) was rerun from `down` with the same seed: after 300 steps every cell is the exact complement (0 mismatched cells), so `up`/`down` and negative-field counterparts are not independent regimes.
- **Finite size and time.** The top set c03 (T 0.84, J 0.6) keeps score 0.534 to 0.585 on 64², 128² and 256² (the low value is the third capture on 64²) and 0.583 at 3000 steps. The runner-up c01 (same T/J, random start) is stable at 128² to 900 steps (0.570, 0.579, 0.561) but drops to 0.209 at 3000 steps and to 0.357 at the third capture on 64²: the lattice coarsens into a two-domain strip (|m| 0.87 at 3000 steps) once the domains span the periodic box. That is a finite-size and finite-time effect on a cold ordered state, not a critical one. c04 (T 2.38, J 1.4, ratio 1.7) is the most size-stable finalist (0.551 to 0.582 across 64², 128² and 256² and 0.582 at 3000).
- **Seeds.** c03 0.580 (seed 7), 0.571 (11), 0.580 (23); c01 0.570, 0.569, 0.577; c04 0.565, 0.570, 0.557. The seed spread is at most 0.013, so the ordering among finalists is within seed noise.
- **Visual finding.** c04 at step 300 (renderer) shows large smooth domains with fine speckle on their boundaries, noticeably smoother than the critical-domains reference, which shows fractal clusters over many scales and a dense speckle; the long-horizon c01 frame is two black bands on a white field with a few isolated flipped cells. The best-scoring states are therefore cold, coarsened domain pictures, the same conclusion as the 2026-08 sweep, and the structure that makes critical Ising interesting (clusters at every scale) scores lower, not higher.
- **Pathological output.** No non-finite values. Endpoint fraction is 1.00 on every row by construction: the field is two-valued (0 or 1).

### Proposed promotions

**None.** The highest-scoring sets are cold, ordered states (T/J 1.4 to 2.0) that coarsen toward stripes and are the same finding as the 2026-08 sweep; the near-critical sets that look most like critical Ising score lower. No `up` start is worth keeping. The shipped critical default stays; this is an explicit no-promotion outcome.
