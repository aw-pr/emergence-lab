## Finding 2: "Maze-like" scores 0.082, and the metric is wrong about it

B3/S12345 at seed 0.05 stabilises into a static maze: coverage 0.539, temporal
flux 0.0006, spatial autocorrelation **−0.07**. The negative autocorrelation is
the whole story — a maze of one-cell-wide corridors alternating with one-cell
walls is anti-correlated at lag 1, which is exactly the high-frequency signal the
structure term is built to punish (it is the same term that starves white noise).

That is a limitation of a lag-1 coherence measure, not a defect in the preset.
The maze looks like a maze; it simply lives at a spatial frequency the metric
cannot see as structure. **Not promoted, and not escalated as a defect** — it is
recorded here so a future sweep does not "fix" a working preset on this number.

A multi-lag or FFT-band structure term would rank fine-scale periodic patterns
correctly. Out of scope for this stage; noted as follow-up.

## Appendix: multi-lag structure term — 2026-08-30

Stage 66 built `multiLagSpatialAutocorrelation` in `e2e/harness/metrics.ts`: the
existing covariance/variance construction generalised to lags {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8},
reporting the strongest (most positive) reading found at any of them. It is
reported beside `spatialAutocorrelation`, not folded into the composite.

Re-running "Maze-like" (B3/S12345, seed 0.05) through the harness:

| term | reading |
|---|---|
| lag-1 (existing) | **−0.0733** |
| multi-lag (new) | **0.3838** |

The rescue is real: at lag 2, corridor cells share the same phase of the
alternation the lag-1 term reads as anti-correlated, so the maze's periodic
structure is visible to the new term even though its fundamental period makes
lag 1 negative.

**The term still starves white noise.** A synthetic uniform-random field
(mulberry32 seed 42, 128×128, i.i.d. draws in [0, 1)) scores:

| term | reading |
|---|---|
| lag-1 (existing) | −0.0013 |
| multi-lag (new) | **0.0075** |

Both numbers are low — the new term has not simply traded a blind spot at lag 1
for a blind spot at every lag simultaneously. Both criteria (rescue Maze-like,
starve noise) hold at once, so this is not the negative-result case the card's
escalation clause anticipated.

Re-scoring the other two shipped references under both terms for comparison:
Conway (S2–3, seed 0.28) reads 0.4896 under both terms; Dense ash (S2–3, seed
0.4) reads 0.5013 under both terms. Neither preset has fine-scale periodic
structure at lag 1, so the new term agrees with the old one exactly for both —
only Maze-like's reading moves. See the cross-sim tabulation in
`docs/sweeps/ising-model-interestingness.md` and
`docs/sweeps/gray-scott-interestingness.md` for the same appendix on sims where
the term was expected to (and does) agree with the existing one.

This card makes no promotion and no composite change (`git diff
src/app/presets.ts` is empty) — it characterises a new instrument, it does not
adopt it. Whether the composite should incorporate a multi-lag term is a
follow-up decision with its own re-scoring plan.

### Interpretation and proposals

The highest new candidate is B2/S23 at sparkRate0.075, seedDensity 0.28, ageShading true. The384² Renderer frames show dense fine-grained agitation with changing local clusters and little large-scale organisation. This is a rejected evidence selection, not a proposed replacement for Conway or Dense ash. Its high flux does not establish richer dynamics: its multi-lag structure is only about0.107 on the128² mean. The same B2/S23 rule at spark 0/0.025/0.2 occupies the next three ranks, with mean scores within0.002 of the leader. This small deterministic score gap is no basis for declaring0.075 an aesthetic optimum.

Conway and Dense ash keep sparse separated colonies and local activity; the new candidate is visually much busier but loses that separation. Maze-like remains a fine corridor pattern with very low temporal flux. Its positive multi-lag structure survives the legacy lag1 penalty; a low composite is not a reason to discard it. The census preserves other rule motifs, but these unvalidated rows support no app-scale promotion claim. Life's age shading and ghosts affect scalar coverage/entropy; the reported coverage is not the fraction of live cells. Neither the initial soup nor the spark hash exposes a seed parameter, so no independent-seed claim is made.

**Proposed promotions: none.** Keep all three shipped references. This bounded birth/survival/spark grid did not establish an app-scale improvement; it does not rule out other birth rules, seed densities or user-preferred patterns. Production presets, the composite and the existing multi-lag calibration remain unchanged.
