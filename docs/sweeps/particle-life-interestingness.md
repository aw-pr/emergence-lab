## The headline: this metric set cannot rank Particle Life

The 2026-07-16 Gray-Scott write-up recorded the same result for boids: *"the
occupancy-field lens is the wrong instrument for a sparse swarm — binary point
density carries almost no spatial-autocorrelation signal."* Particle Life is a
point-cloud sim rasterised the same way, and it fails the same way.

**Spatial autocorrelation is between −0.007 and +0.033 for all 27 swept sets and
all 3 references.** Structure is 55% of the composite, so 55% of the score is a
constant ≈0 for every candidate. Across the swept sets entropy moves only within
0.192–0.194 and coverage within 0.165–0.169. The swept sets span 0.087 to 0.105
and the references 0.080 to 0.090 — a total spread of 0.025, dominated by the
third decimal of the coverage term rather than by anything the sim is doing.

This was checked before concluding it, not assumed: the sweep was re-probed at
64², 96², 128² and 256² grids and at 1 000–5 000 particles. Autocorrelation
stayed at ≈0 in every combination. A one-cell dot next to an empty cell is
maximally anti-correlated at lag 1 no matter how the dots are clustered, so
clumping — the thing that makes Particle Life worth watching — is invisible to a
lag-1 coherence measure.

The sweep was still run in full, and the artefacts and ranking are recorded
below, because a negative result that has been measured is worth more than an
untested assumption.

## Harness settings

128×128 grid, channel 0 (Red species density), 150 warmup steps, flux gap 6,
coverage threshold 0.05.

`particleCount` is pinned at 3 000 for swept sets **and references**. The shipped
presets run 5 000–12 000 on the app's 640² grid; 3 000 on 128² is the matching
order of density. So this sweep cannot rank the shipped presets on population,
and none of its numbers would justify a population change.

## Promotion

**None, and this is a recorded null result rather than an escalation.** The top
swept set nominally beats the best incumbent by 0.015, but on a metric that has
been demonstrated blind to this sim that number carries no information. Promoting
on it would be worse than not sweeping at all.

Follow-up, if Particle Life is to be tuned by measurement: score a *smoothed*
density field (a Gaussian blur at the interaction radius before the metrics run),
or score the species-mixing statistics directly — how often unlike species are
adjacent — which is what the attraction matrix actually controls. Both mean a
new metric, which the stage card puts out of scope. The same follow-up would
serve boids.
