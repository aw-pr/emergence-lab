# Kuramoto oscillators

A crowd can begin in disorder and still find a shared rhythm. Fireflies flash together, cardiac cells pulse together, and generators on an electrical grid hold a common frequency. The Kuramoto model strips that transition down to phases, natural frequencies, and coupling.

Each oscillator advances at its own preferred rate. Coupling adds a correction based on the phase difference between neighbours. Weak coupling leaves the field incoherent. Stronger coupling gathers oscillators into moving phase bands, vortices, or a globally locked rhythm.

The local mode couples each cell to its four lattice neighbours, making spatial waves visible. The global mode couples every oscillator through the population's mean phase. The colour wheel is cyclic: neighbouring colours represent neighbouring phases, including across the wrap from one back to zero.

The interesting region is the threshold. Synchronisation does not arrive one oscillator at a time in a visually uniform way. Coherent groups recruit one another until a macroscopic rhythm becomes possible. A collective pattern appears without a conductor.
